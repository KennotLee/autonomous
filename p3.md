# P3 · Delay risk model: write-up

**Task:** for each ATL/DFW flight, give the probability that it arrives more than 45 minutes late, 3–12 hours ahead of time. Compare it with simple baselines, report honest metrics, choose and justify an alert threshold, and explain one failure case.

**Status:** done and pushed on branch `p3`, not merged to `main`. Code is in `src/models/delay/`, outputs are in `outputs/`.

---

## 1. Label (P1's)
`label_45 = ARR_DELAY > 45` (1/0). It's left blank for cancelled (345) and diverted (78) flights, which are not trained on or scored but still get a prediction. The base rate is 10.7% overall and 13.6% in the 2025 test period.

## 2. Data
| Source | What it gives | Why |
|---|---|---|
| P1 `flight_features.parquet` (branch `p1`) | 25,299 ATL/DFW flights × h ∈ {3,6,9,12}; weather at both ends as of t − h; `label_45`; same-hour `oracle_*` weather | the team contract |
| Full BTS on-time data, 2023-01..2025-07 (public, `python -m pipeline.download`) | every ATL/DFW flight, used only to build hub-level features | the team CSV is a ~0.5% sample (~6 flights per hub per day), too thin to measure congestion |

The CSV's own weather columns are not used: P1 found them misaligned by 4–6 hours.

## 3. Features (all leakage-safe)
The forecast time t is the scheduled departure (UTC), following P1. Every input must be known at t − h.

- **From P1:** weather at the origin and destination as of t − h (temperature, dew point, precipitation, wind, pressure change, 3 h and 6 h windows, condition code). Headwind, crosswind and tailwind at the hub end, against runway headings 080° (ATL) and 180° (DFW). Carrier, distance, block time and calendar.
- **Added by P3:**
  - **Thunderstorm and heavy-precipitation flags** at each end, derived from P1's `coco`.
  - **Hub schedule congestion:** scheduled arrivals and departures at ATL/DFW in the hour the flight uses the hub. The schedule is published in advance, so this is allowed.
  - **Hub state** in the last *complete* hour before t − h: share of scheduled arrivals that actually landed, share delayed > 45 min, cancellation rate, mean arrival and departure delay (pooled over 3 h).
- **Never used as features:** `oracle_*` (oracle model only), `delay_min`, `label_45`, `cancelled`, `diverted`, and the BTS delay-cause columns.

## 4. Models and baselines
- **Split by scheduled departure:**
  - **Train:** before 2024-07.
  - **Validation:** 2024-07..12, used for early stopping and to choose the threshold.
  - **Test:** 2025-01..07.
  - No random splitting.
- **Model:** one LightGBM classifier per horizon, as P1's README asks.
- **Compared against:**
  - **Base rate:** a constant.
  - **Weather rule:** high risk if, at either end within the 3 h before t − h, there is a thunderstorm or heavy-precipitation code, a gust > 25 kt, or sustained wind > 20 kt. Gusts are missing in ~97% of hours, so the sustained-wind clause does most of the work.
  - **Climatology:** training-period rate by hub × inbound/outbound × local arrival hour, smoothed.
  - **`gbm_p1_only`:** the same model with P1's features only, to show what the P3 hub features add.
  - **`gbm_oracle`:** the model plus same-hour weather. This information isn't available at forecast time; the gap shows what a perfect weather forecast would be worth.

## 5. Results (test: 5,641 flights × 4 horizons, base rate 13.6%)
| Model | PR-AUC | Brier | Precision | Recall | Flagged |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Base rate | 0.136 | 0.119 | – | – | – |
| Weather rule | 0.148 | 0.202 | 0.21 | 0.17 | 11% |
| Climatology | 0.189 | 0.116 | 0.21 | 0.28 | 19% |
| Model, P1 features only | 0.277 | 0.110 | 0.28 | 0.45 | 22% |
| **Model** | **0.303** | **0.108** | **0.29** | **0.48** | 23% |
| Oracle (same-hour weather) | 0.346 | 0.105 | 0.30 | 0.51 | 23% |

**PR-AUC by horizon:**

| h | Climatology | P1 features only | Model | Oracle |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 3 h | 0.19 | 0.29 | **0.34** | 0.37 |
| 6 h | 0.19 | 0.28 | **0.31** | 0.36 |
| 9 h | 0.19 | 0.28 | **0.28** | 0.34 |
| 12 h | 0.19 | 0.27 | **0.27** | 0.32 |

- **By hub:** ATL 0.29 vs 0.18 for climatology; DFW 0.32 vs 0.20.
- **The model beats every baseline at every horizon.**
- **Hub features matter most at short horizons:** they add +0.05 PR-AUC at 3 h and almost nothing at 12 h, because the hub's state 12 hours earlier says little.
- **Calibration** (`outputs/metrics/delay_calibration.png`): predicted probabilities match observed rates, so "20%" means about 1 in 5.
- **Top drivers at h = 6:** hub departure delay, carrier, departure hour, origin pressure and dew point, month, hub severe-delay share.

## 6. Decision threshold: 0.15
Chosen as the maximum F1 on the 2024 H2 validation data (validation precision 0.30, recall 0.42), so a missed severe delay and a false alert count equally. On test this flags 23% of flights and catches 48% of severe delays, with 29% of alerts correct (2.1× the base rate).

A coordinator who wants fewer alerts can move along the trade-off:

| Threshold | Precision | Recall | Flights flagged |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0.10 | 0.23 | 0.66 | 39% |
| **0.15** | **0.29** | **0.48** | **23%** |
| 0.20 | 0.33 | 0.35 | 15% |
| 0.25 | 0.38 | 0.25 | 9% |
| 0.30 | 0.42 | 0.16 | 5% |

## 7. Failure case: ATL, Sunday 11 May 2025 (h = 6)
This was auto-selected as the test day with the most severe delays the model missed. Write-up and chart: `outputs/metrics/delay_failure_case.md` / `.png`.

- **What happened:** fog and light rain from 06:00, solid overcast, an ENE wind of 10–14 kt, heavy rain at 18:00–19:00, and no thunderstorm. 7 of the 29 sampled flights were more than 45 min late, up to +226 min. The 6-hour model put **all 7 below 0.15** (8% mean over the day).
- **Why:**
  - **Wind:** it was a headwind on the 080° runway, so the wind features looked fine.
  - **Rain:** rain without thunder is usually harmless at ATL, so the model learned to ignore it.
  - **The likely real cause:** low cloud and poor visibility (instrument conditions), and our data has neither: visibility is 100% missing at the hubs and cloud base isn't provided. This is inferred from the weather codes; I did not check it against FAA records.
  - **Slow build-up:** six hours before the evening flights, the hub was only mildly late (~13–16 min).
- **How a user would notice:**
  - The **3-hour forecast flagged** the 17:00–19:00 departures (0.24–0.30), driven by ATL departure delays of 51–61 min and arrival capacity down to 75–80%.
  - When the top drivers switch from calendar terms to hub state, older long-horizon forecasts are stale.
  - A low probability means "no signal yet", not "safe": the coordinator still reads the TAF cloud base and visibility.
- **Fix:** add TAF or METAR cloud base and visibility.

## 8. Limitations (say these on the slide)
- **Thin sample:** the team CSV is a ~0.5% sample, so the test set is ~5.6k flights and per-day analysis is thin. Hub features come from full BTS to compensate.
- **Weather gaps:** no visibility or cloud base; gusts missing in ~97% of hours; `coco` is model-filled by Meteostat.
- **Runway direction:** one fixed heading per airport, with no east/west flow changes and no magnetic-vs-true correction.
- **Cancellations and diversions:** not counted as "severe", because P1's label leaves them blank. That likely understates the risk on the worst days.
- **Test period:** January–July 2025 only. Its delay rate (13.6%) is higher than 2023–24 (9–10%).

## 10. How to reproduce
```
git show origin/p1:data/processed/flight_features.parquet > data/processed/flight_features.parquet
python -m pipeline.download          # optional: full BTS + hub weather, for hub features and the failure chart
python -m src.models.delay.train     # ~1 min, deterministic
```
Requirements beyond `requirements.txt`: `lightgbm`, `scikit-learn`, `matplotlib`, `tabulate`, and `meteostat>=2` for `pipeline.download`. `requirements.txt` pins 1.7.6.
